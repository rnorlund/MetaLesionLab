# Prisoners of the cave: does a model that has seen pictures draw a better chair than its text-only twin? Pre-registered plan

Written and committed on 2026-10-28, before any chair, clock or description was generated by the two vision twins, and before any clock or description was generated by the two text twins.

What happened before this commit:
- The four models' licences and revisions were checked on the Hugging Face Hub, and their weights were downloaded to axon-server03.
- Each vision twin was loaded once and asked "Reply with the single word: ok" (2 answers each), to check that it loads (the 32B in FP8) and answers a text-only turn. No chair, clock or description was asked for.
- The text twins' chair answers already exist: they are the 7B and 32B rows of the model-size arm of `docs/plato_scale.md`, which are already public. They are reused (see "Parity").

## Question

In Plato's cave, the prisoners know only shadows. A language model trained only on text knows chairs only from words: shadows of the real thing.

On `/plato/scale`, text-only open models (Qwen2.5 0.5B to 32B, OLMo 2, Qwen3) asked to "draw a simple chair as an SVG drawing" drew boxes and tables, rarely chairs. Meta's Muse Spark and Muse Wonder, which have seen images, drew chairs. But those models differ in many ways: maker, size, data, tuning, reasoning.

The clean test is **twins that differ in whether they were trained with pictures.** Qwen2.5-VL's language model starts from the Qwen2.5 language model's weights and is then trained further on pictures with text, and on text (Bai et al. 2025, *Qwen2.5-VL Technical Report*, arXiv:2502.13923). So Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct are twins: same maker, same base model, same size class, same tokenizer family. One has seen pictures; the other has only read.

**Does the twin that has seen pictures draw a recognizable chair more often than the twin that has only read? And do both describe a chair equally well in words?** "They know the word; can they draw the thing?"

## Tasks

12 answers per model, task and temperature.

| Task | Request (one user turn, no system prompt of ours) | Temperatures |
|---|---|---|
| **Chair** | *Draw a simple chair as an SVG drawing: black strokes only on a white background, 256 by 256 pixels, no text, no colour fill. Output only the SVG code.* (the scale experiment's request, word for word) | **1.0 (primary)**, 0.7 (check) |
| **Clock** | *Draw a clock showing 10 past 11 as an SVG drawing: black strokes only on a white background, 256 by 256 pixels, no colour fill. Output only the SVG code.* ("no text" is dropped so a clock may carry numbers) | 1.0 |
| **Describe** | *Describe what a chair looks like in two sentences.* | 1.0 |

**Sampling** (the scale experiment's, `scripts/plato_scale_svg.py`): pure temperature sampling (top-p 1, no top-k, no repetition penalty), at most 2,048 new tokens, one batch of 12 per task and temperature (halved only on out-of-memory), torch seed 20261028 + 100 × temperature (+1000 per extra batch). Each model's own chat template, including any default system prompt it adds.

**Parity.** The text twins' chair answers at both temperatures are reused from the scale experiment. They used the same request, sampling, seed rule, revisions and precision (BF16 for 7B, FP8 for 32B), on an L40S. Everything else (both vision twins' three tasks, both text twins' clocks and descriptions) is generated here, on axon-server03 (one L40S, 46 GB), with `scripts/plato_twins_generate.py`.

## Measures

Every SVG goes through the scale experiment's code unchanged (`metalesionlab/plato_scale.py`): the first complete `<svg>…</svg>`, rendered by cairosvg at 256 × 256 on white with files and network blocked. A **failure** is no SVG, a renderer error, a blank canvas (< 0.1% ink) or one covered in ink (> 95%).

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Valid drawing** | Not a failure, out of 12. |
| **Read as a chair (primary)** | Google SigLIP 2 (`google/siglip2-so400m-patch14-384`), zero-shot: of the 35 Plato's Chair names ("a simple line drawing of a chair", …), "chair" fits best. Out of all 12 answers: a failure is not a chair. The reader, template and cropping are the scale experiment's (`scripts/plato_scale_reader.py`). |
| Recognizable (DINOv2, secondary) | The drawing's nearest of the 35 Quick, Draw! concept centroids in Meta DINOv2 (base, CLS) is the chair's; out of 12. The scale experiment's pre-registered measure. |
| **Spread** | Mean pairwise DINOv2 cosine distance among a model's rendered drawings (at least 4), with a bootstrap CI; also divided by people's spread at the same number of drawings (200 random subsets of the 200 Quick, Draw! reference chairs), as in the scale experiment. |
| Chair by eye (secondary) | Coded blind (below): would a person name this drawing a chair? 0/1, out of 12 (a failure is 0). |
| **Clock** (exploratory) | Valid drawing; read as a clock by SigLIP 2 (as above, "clock" among the 35 names); and by eye, blind: **face** (a closed dial outline), **hands** (two hands from the centre), **time** "11:10" if a shorter hand points at the 11 or a little past it (less than halfway to the 12) and a longer hand points at the 2, "other" if it shows another time, "unclear" if the hands can't be told apart or there are none. Failures count as 0 / "unclear". |
| **Description** | Rubric, coded by eye, blind: does it mention the **seat**, the **legs**, the **back**? 0/1 each; score 0–3. A word or a plain paraphrase counts ("a surface to sit on" is a seat; "something to lean against" is a back). |

**Blind coding and the hash.**
1. After generation, failures are classified and drawings rendered (both objective, needed to see them).
2. `scripts/plato_twins_measure.py sheets` writes three coding sheets: the 48 descriptions, the 48 chairs at temperature 1.0, and the 48 clocks, each in a fixed shuffled order (seed 20261029) under anonymous ids. Which model made which item is in a separate key file that the coder doesn't open.
3. Roger's agent (Claude) codes every item on the sheets by eye. Its codes are saved as JSON.
4. The SHA-256 of each codes file (canonical JSON) is written to `docs/plato_cave_twins_codes.sha256` and **committed before any automatic scoring**: before SigLIP 2 reads any of the new drawings, before DINOv2 measures are computed, and before the keyword check of the descriptions.
5. Only then is the key joined, and the automatic measures run.

The coder is not naive (it wrote this plan and knows the prediction), but it cannot see which model made which item.

**Keyword check of the descriptions** (run after the hash): a part counts if its word appears (`seat`/`sit`, `leg`/`legs`, `back`/`backrest`). Agreement with the by-eye codes is reported per part (percent and Cohen's kappa). The by-eye codes are primary.

## Predictions (made now)

- **P1. The twin that has seen pictures draws chairs that are read as chairs more often than its text-only twin, at both sizes** (SigLIP 2, out of 12, temperature 1.0).
- **P2. Both twins describe a chair equally well in words** (the rubric).

Expectations without a verdict (exploratory): the vision twins also produce more valid drawings, more clocks read as clocks and more clocks showing 11:10, and more chairs that look like chairs by eye. No prediction is made for spread.

## Statistics and the verdict rule

- **Bootstrap:** 2,000 replicates, seed 20261029, resampling each model's 12 answers independently; the difference is vision twin − text twin; 95% percentile interval.
- **P1 at one size:** **supported** if the CI of the difference in "read as a chair" lies above 0; **contradicted** if below 0; otherwise **unclear**.
- **P1 overall:** **supported** if supported at both sizes; **partly supported** if at one size and not contradicted at the other; **contradicted** if contradicted at either; otherwise **unclear**.
- **P2 at one size:** **supported** if both twins' mean rubric score is at least 2 of 3 ("both know the word") **and** the CI of the difference in mean score lies within ±0.5 points; **not supported** if either twin scores below 2; otherwise **unclear**. **P2 overall:** supported if supported at both sizes.
- Secondary: a one-sided Fisher exact test of "read as a chair" per size, reported beside the bootstrap, never instead of it.
- With 12 answers per model, the bootstrap CI of a 0-of-12 against k-of-12 difference excludes 0 only from about k = 4. A small advantage would read as "unclear". That is the honest limit of 12 answers.
