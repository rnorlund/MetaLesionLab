What would Plato ask an AI? Damaged AI: we damaged AIs on purpose, starting with Meta’s own open model, Muse Glimmer. We switched off some of their artificial neurons, or added random noise to them.

What happens to an AI’s idea of a thing when we damage it?

Damage Meta’s open AI, and its drawings go first: it can still say what a chair is after it can no longer draw one.

How we know

Meta’s open AI model, Muse Glimmer, is one anyone can download and run. We ran it on our own graphics card and switched off a share of its artificial neurons, step by step, from none to 30%. At every step we asked it to draw a chair, a clock and God as drawing code, and to describe each in one sentence, 12 times each: 36 answers per step. A separate AI, Google’s SigLIP 2, looked at each drawing and said whether it showed the right thing.

We predicted before the run that the drawing would go first, and it did (details). Much of that is drawings becoming unrecognisable before they stop coming out at all. The damage is to an AI, so this is a comparison, not a claim about people.

A second AI: damage the part that reads words, and a rocking chair becomes a car

Meta’s image AI, Muse Image, runs only on Meta’s servers, so we can’t damage it. Stable Diffusion XL is an open image AI with two parts, one that reads your words and one that paints the picture, so we damaged each part.

How we know

Plato thought every real chair copies one perfect, ideal chair, which he called its Form. An image AI seems to hold an idea like that too: ask it for a rocking chair and it knows what to draw. So we damaged image AIs on purpose, one part at a time, to see what that idea is made of.

The pictures: Stable Diffusion XL, an open image AI from Stability AI, was asked for a rocking chair every time, with the same random starting point. Each step adds more random noise to the artificial neurons of one part (steps 1 to 3 of 4 shown). Under each picture is the word that Meta’s open AI model, Muse Glimmer (one anyone can download and look inside), used for it. Damage to the part that reads the words turned the rocking chair into a car, and God into an ordinary person.

Our predictions held only in part: see what we predicted.

Brain science learned much of what each part of the brain does by studying people whose brains were damaged in one place. Our damage is to an AI, and we can undo it. It is a comparison, not a claim about people.
