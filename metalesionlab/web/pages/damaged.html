What happens to an AI’s idea of a thing when we damage it?

Plato thought every real chair copies one perfect, ideal chair, which he called its Form. An image AI seems to hold an idea like that too: ask it for a rocking chair and it knows what to draw. So we damaged image AIs on purpose, one part at a time, to see what that idea is made of.

Brain science learned much of what each part of the brain does by studying people whose brains were damaged in one place. Our damage is to an AI, and we can undo it. It is a comparison, not a claim about people.

Damage the part of an image AI that reads the words and it forgets what it is drawing: a rocking chair becomes a car, and God becomes an ordinary person. Damage the part that paints the picture and it mostly forgets how to draw.

Our predictions held only in part: see what we predicted. It is a comparison, not a claim about people.
