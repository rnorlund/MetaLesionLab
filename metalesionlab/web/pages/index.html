Language testing is slow, locked up, and mostly English.

Materials are scarce

Standard picture tests are copyrighted and built for English. Making a new set by hand, with checked pictures and agreed names, takes weeks.

Scoring takes hours

Every response is transcribed and coded by hand: was it right, a related word, a sound error, a made-up word? One session can take longer to score than to give.

The mechanism is hidden

We see which errors people make, but can't open a brain to test which damage causes which error. In an AI model, we can.

How it works

  1. 1List wordsType them in any language; pin a meaning when a word has two.
  2. 2DrawMuse Image draws several candidates per word.
  3. 3CheckSAM 3.1 confirms one clear object; Muse Spark confirms the name.
  4. 4GiveShow pictures; responses are recorded and transcribed.
  5. 5ScoreRules code each response; Muse Spark gives a second opinion.
  6. 6Review and exportA person confirms the codes; download the table.

Tasks: picture naming, picture description and category fluency. Every automatic code goes to a person for review before export.

Who it's for

Language researchers

Aphasia, dementia, child language and bilingualism studies that need normed materials and consistent scoring.

Clinicians in training

Practice scoring with instant feedback, using pictures you are free to print and share.

Teachers

Show students how different kinds of damage produce different errors, live in class.

AI researchers

Test models with tasks from cognitive science and locate where words form inside a model.

Any language Muse Voice Transcribe covers 25 languages. Tested end to end so far: spoken English and Spanish; Muse Spark also named test pictures correctly in Hindi and Swahili. Other languages are marked experimental until checked.

Responsible by design

No diagnosis

Tip of the Tongue produces error codes for review. It does not diagnose or give medical or treatment advice.

A person decides

Every automatic code can be changed by the reviewer, and exports keep both.

Your data stays yours

Use speaker codes, never names. Audio is sent for transcription and not stored by the app. Don’t upload identifiable patient recordings without approval.

Open and inspectable

Apache 2.0 source, documented scoring rules, and a cited source for every human comparison.
