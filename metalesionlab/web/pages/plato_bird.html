Plato’s Bird

Ask 20 people to name a bird and you get 11 different birds. Ask Meta’s Muse Spark the same question 50 times.

90%

Across 117 categories, Muse Spark’s favourite answer takes 90% of its answers. People’s favourite takes 48%.

Method and caveats

  • People. The Banks & Connell (2023) category production norms: 117 categories (67 concrete, 50 abstract), 20 UK adults per category, each naming aloud as many members as they could in 60 seconds. We use each person’s first answer. Production frequency is how many of the 20 named a member at all; typicality ratings (1–5) came from separate raters.
  • AI. The same question for every category: “Name one member of the category bird. Answer with one word or a short name only.” Muse Spark 1.3: 50 separate calls, default settings, minimal reasoning. Muse Glimmer 30B (open weights, run on our own GPU): its probabilities for the first word of the answer, and 50 samples at temperatures 0.7, 1 and 1.5.
  • Fair comparison. 20 answers look more varied than 50 only by chance, so every share and count is measured at people’s sample size. The plan, measures and predictions were written down and committed before any AI answers were collected.
  • Not the same task. People named members aloud for a minute and we kept their first; the AI answered once per call. People are 20 UK adults, and the AI was likely tuned on American English (it says “soccer”, people say “football”; we merge such pairs).
  • Defaults are defaults. The AI’s narrowness here is what it does when asked once, at its usual settings. Glimmer’s own probabilities are about as spread as people’s answers; how varied its answers look depends on the sampling temperature (see above).
