Seven meanings

    What the prosody reader hears

    The stress the transcript loses is still in the sound: the stressed word is higher, louder and longer. A plain reader, with no AI in it, measures those three things for each word and picks the one that stands out most. Press a recording above to see it here.

    Try it with your voice

    Say “I never said she stole my money”, stressing any one word. Voice Transcribe writes it down, Muse Spark guesses your meaning from the writing alone, and the prosody reader guesses from your voice. Then tell us which word you meant.

    Press Record, say the sentence, then press Stop. 10 seconds at most.

    Your recording is processed in memory and never stored or logged. To write it down and ask Muse Spark, it is sent to Meta’s API (about a third of a cent a try, under the site’s daily budget). The prosody reader runs on our own server.

    How we did it, and what it can’t show

    • The recordings. Thirteen synthetic voices said the sentence eight ways: neutral, and with each word stressed. The four you can play are Kokoro-82M, an open voice that can’t stress a word by itself, so we added the stress the way English speakers mark it: the word higher, louder and longer, and the words after it lower and flatter. Six macOS voices stressed the word with their own pitch, volume and rate commands (their emphasis command did nothing at all). Three eSpeak NG voices used their own emphasis. No recording of a person was used. The macOS and eSpeak clips stay on our computer.
    • The shadow. Every recording went through Meta’s Voice Transcribe (muse-voice-transcribe-1.0) once. …
    • Text only. Muse Spark (muse-spark-1.3) got one fixed question: “Someone said one sentence out loud and stressed one word to give it a particular meaning. Here is the sentence, written down: … Which meaning did the speaker intend?”, with the seven meanings as options in a shuffled order. It saw each transcript, and, as a control, the sentence with the stressed word in CAPITALS or in *italics*.
    • The prosody reader. No AI. It lines the recording up with neutral readings of the sentence to find each word, then measures each word’s pitch peak, loudness peak and length against the rest of the sentence. The most prominent word is its guess. Its settings were fixed before it heard any test recording.
    • Pre-registered. The predictions were committed before the main run (plan). …
    • Limits. The voices are synthetic, and in two of the three families we built the stress ourselves, from the very cues the reader measures, so its success there mostly shows that the stress survives in the sound. eSpeak’s own emphasis, and your voice, are fairer tests. One sentence, seven meanings, our wording of them.

    Voices: Kokoro-82M (hexgrad, Apache-2.0). We made the stress stronger by changing the timing, pitch and loudness Kokoro plans for the stressed word (version 2; the first version used the WORLD voice-editing software, pyworld, MIT).
