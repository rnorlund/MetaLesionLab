The damage zoo

Switch off an image AI’s artificial neurons and each one breaks in its own way. Damage the part that reads the words and some draw strangers; damage the part that draws and the picture dissolves.

Each card follows one picture, drawn from the same random start at every step, so you see what the damage did to it. Each AI’s damage runs from intact to where its pictures stop meaning anything, so the share of neurons switched off differs between AIs; it is under each picture.

How we did it

  • The request. The same one we give Meta’s Muse Image: “A quick simple doodle sketch of a chair, black marker on plain white, like a 20-second drawing, no text, no shading.”
  • The damage. An image AI has two parts: one reads the words, the other turns them into a picture. In one part at a time, we switched off a share of the artificial neurons in every layer (the neurons inside each layer’s feed-forward step), chosen at random. Each step keeps the neurons the step before switched off and adds more, like damage that spreads. Nothing is changed for good: the neurons come back when the picture is done.
  • The range. A first pass went from 0 to 90% of neurons off in steps of 10. Past some point each AI’s pictures stop meaning anything (noise, texture, a blank), and we chose that point by eye for each AI and part. The 11 steps here run from intact to it.
  • Caveats. One random choice of neurons per part, and two pictures per thing per step (one shown): this shows the variety, it measures nothing. Not planned in advance. “Neurons” here are numbers in a program, not brain cells.
